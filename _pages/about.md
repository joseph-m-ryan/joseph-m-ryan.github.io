I'm a PhD candidate in physics at the [Duke Quantum Center](https://quantum.duke.edu/), where I build and run trapped-ion experiments in the [Noel Lab](https://noellab.pratt.duke.edu/), advised by Crystal Noel.

I work on **quantum stochastic processes**: how noise and measurement shape the dynamics of trapped ions. My recent work includes the first experimental measurement of quantum first-passage-time distributions, the quantum–classical crossover in noisy monitored oscillators, and the heating and escape of trapped atoms driven by colored noise.

Alongside my work in the Noel Lab, I collaborate with [Christopher Monroe](https://iontrap.duke.edu/) on noise-driven heating and ion loss, and with [Stephen Teitsworth](https://scholars.duke.edu/person/teitso) on the theory of first-passage times.

Before Duke, I earned a B.S. in Honors Physics & Mathematics from the University of Michigan, where I worked with [Christine Aidala's group](https://websites.umich.edu/~caidala/index.html) on the LHCb experiment at CERN. My senior thesis on QCD color entanglement won the William L. Williams Award for the best undergraduate thesis in physics.
