First-passage-time distributions (FPTDs) describe when a stochastic process first reaches a target state — a classic question in statistical physics with no unique quantum analogue, since quantum mechanics allows for superpositions. This project develops both the experimental and theoretical toolkit for measuring and understanding these quantum first-passage-time distributions (QFPTDs).

In [*Experimental measurement of quantum first-passage-time distributions*](/publication/2026-05-07-quantum-first-passage-time-distributions) (Phys. Rev. Research 8, L022025), we performed the first measurement of a QFPTD, using a novel composite-phase pulse sequence to stroboscopically measure the motional energy of a single trapped ion.

In the companion theory paper, [*Quantum-classical crossover in noisy monitored oscillators*](/publication/2026-08-08-quantum-classical-crossover-noisy-monitored-oscillators) (arXiv:2608.08267), we studied where quantum and classical first-passage statistics diverge and where they merge, and found that measurement-conditioned states retain nonclassical signatures — such as Wigner-function negativity — even once the observed timing statistics themselves look classical.
