A trapped atom or ion sits in a potential of finite depth. Near the bottom the motion is almost harmonic, but the confinement softens toward the escape energy, so the oscillation frequency drops as the particle heats. When the noise driving it is *colored* — its strength depends on frequency — the heating rate changes along the way, and the usual white-noise picture of escape no longer applies. This project asks how long a particle survives in that setting, and what its lifetime can tell us about the noise it sees.

In [*Heating and Escape of Confined Atoms Subject to Colored Noise*](/publication/2026-10-06-heating-escape-confined-atoms-colored-noise) (arXiv:2610.09047), we studied a particle in a softening trap driven by noise with a power-law spectrum. In the weak-noise regime, where the particle oscillates many times before escaping and the noise never appreciably tilts the trap, we used stochastic averaging to derive the mean escape time and checked it against Monte Carlo simulations of the full dynamics. Outside that regime, large and slowly varying forces open additional escape routes.

Comparing with measured ion lifetimes in a microfabricated trap, we found that the color of the noise alone cannot account for the fast loss observed, whereas a stray field of plausible size tilting the barrier could. The results apply to trapped atoms in noisy force fields and, more broadly, to nonlinear oscillators driven by colored noise.
