excerpt: "How power-law colored noise heats a trapped particle out of a softening potential, and whether noise color can explain rapid ion loss in a microfabricated trap."
abstract: >-
  A particle held in a finite-depth trap, such as a trapped atom or ion, moves almost harmonically at
  low energy, but its confining potential softens as it nears the escape energy. We study how long
  such a particle survives when it is driven by noise with a power-law spectrum. In the weak-noise
  regime, where the particle oscillates many times before escaping and the noise never appreciably
  tilts the trap, we use stochastic averaging to derive the mean escape time and compare it with
  Monte Carlo simulations of the full dynamics. We also examine what happens outside this regime,
  where large, slowly varying forces open additional escape routes. Comparing with measured ion
  lifetimes in a microfabricated trap, we find that the color of the noise alone cannot account for
  the fast loss observed, whereas a stray field of plausible size tilting the barrier could. The
  results apply to trapped atoms in noisy force fields and, more broadly, to nonlinear oscillators
  driven by colored noise.
